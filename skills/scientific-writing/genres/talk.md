# Genre: talk

**Reader**: a general scientific audience that hears each sentence once
and cannot reread.

The ledger has one row per slide, and its claim cell is the slide's
title.

## Rules

- **One key idea.** Peyton Jones 2016: "if you remember nothing else,
  remember this". Name it in the ledger before any slide exists; every
  slide either supports it or is cut.
- **The opening answers Heilmeier questions 1 to 4** with no jargon:
  what you are trying to do, how it is done today and its limits, what
  is new and why it will work, and who cares. Check the opening with
  And-But-Therefore: context *and* context, *but* the gap, *therefore*
  this work. An opening that is all "and" has no problem to solve.
- **Assertion titles over visual evidence** (Alley's assertion-evidence
  approach). Each content slide's title is a full-sentence claim of at
  most two lines, and the body is the figure or equation that supports
  it. A topic label ("Results", "Convergence") is not a title.
- **Examples before the general case.** Show one concrete instance, then
  generalise.
- **Omit** an outline slide first, a related-work survey, and apologies
  for time or polish.
- **Deliberate repetition: message, evidence, message.** This inverts
  the paper rule. A reader who misses a sentence rereads it; a listener
  who misses it has lost it, so the claim is stated before the evidence
  and again after it.
- **Heilmeier questions 5 and 8** -- risks, and the mid-term and final
  exams -- belong only in group meetings and defenses.
- **Speaker notes carry what the slide must not**: derivation steps,
  caveats, exact values the slide omits.

## Evidence routing on a slide

The visibility question is stricter: the audience gets seconds, not
minutes, and reads from the back of the room. A figure that needs a
caption to be understood fails it -- enlarge, annotate, or cut panels
until the claim is visible at a glance.

## Budgets

No paragraph budget applies to slides. A slide carries one claim in its
title; body text beyond labels and annotations moves to the notes.
